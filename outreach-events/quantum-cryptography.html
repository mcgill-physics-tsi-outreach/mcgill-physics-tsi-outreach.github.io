Quantum Cryptography Workshop

October 14, 2025

On October 14, 2025, McGill Physics Outreach hosted a Quantum Cryptography Workshop for undergraduate students at McGill University. The event aimed to introduce students to the fascinating world of quantum mechanics and its applications in secure communication.

The workshop began with an engaging introduction to the fundamentals of quantum physics, inviting students to explore big questions such as What is quantum? and What is light? Through interactive demonstrations, they discovered how the polarization of light can be used to represent a qubit—the basic unit of quantum information. Students experimented with the ideas of superposition and measurement disturbance, gaining insight into how observing a quantum system can change its state. This hands-on exploration highlighted the key differences between the classical and quantum pictures of light, and taught students these concepts form the basis of quantum cryptography. .

Next, students dove into the world of quantum cryptography through an introduction to Quantum Key Distribution (QKD). They learned how two fundamental principles—measurement disturbance and the no-cloning theorem—form the backbone of secure quantum communication. Using the well-known BB84 protocol as a guide, participants explored how quantum states can be used to generate secret keys that are inherently protected from eavesdropping, which gave students a glimpse into how quantum mechanics is transforming the future of secure information transfer.

Following the presentation, in the final part of the workshop, students stepped into the roles of Alice and Bob. Using 3D-printed laser kits, they exchanged secret keys and saw firsthand how the rules of quantum physics prevent eavesdroppers from going undetected. Working in small teams, participants generated their own quantum-secure key and used it to transmit a binary message, bringing the concepts of QKD to life through a hands-on activity.

Throughout the day, students had the opportunity to ask questions and discuss the implications of quantum technologies, with the workshop also highlighting current advancements in quantum computing and its potential impact on various industries.

We would like to thank Fiona Thompson, Scientific Outreach Officer from the Institute of Quantum Computing at the University Waterloo, for leading such a great workshop!
