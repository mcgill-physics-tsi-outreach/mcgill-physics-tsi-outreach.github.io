McGill Physics Outreach Brings Hands-On Science to Barbados

Bellairs Research Institute • Ages 10–14 • One-day outreach program

Catherine Boisvert and Valentina Mazzotti, Outreach Coordinators with the McGill Physics Outreach Committee, recently represented McGill at the Bellairs Research Institute in Barbados, where they led a full day of hands-on physics activities for more than 80 students aged 10 to 14. The event was part of the island’s We Gatherin’ celebration and brought together students from local middle and high schools for an exciting introduction to the world of physics.

“It was inspiring to see the students’ curiosity and enthusiasm for physics and to help spark their interest in future STEM careers,” said Mazzotti.

Morning: Curiosity in Motion

  • Potato Lamp: Students learned how two different metals inserted into a potato can create a small voltage to light an LED—an accessible way to demonstrate electrochemistry and energy conversion.
  • Parachute Challenge: Teams designed and tested mini parachutes, exploring how air resistance and gravity influence motion.
  • Sink or Float: Through predictions and tests, students discovered how density and buoyancy determine whether objects float—complete with the classic fresh- vs. salt-water egg demo.

Afternoon: Building and Experimenting

  • Build a Simple Electric Motor: Learners saw how electric current and magnetic fields produce motion—the principle behind everyday motors.
  • Make a Compass: By magnetizing a needle and floating it on water, students observed alignment with Earth’s magnetic field.
  • Potato Lamp (Series Cells): Older students connected multiple cells and measured how voltage adds in series, linking science to simple engineering.

Inspiring the Next Generation

Throughout the day, the atmosphere was filled with excitement, curiosity, and collaboration. Students asked thoughtful questions, shared their results, and proudly showed off their creations. The outreach visit at Bellairs reflects McGill Physics’ ongoing commitment to making science accessible and engaging beyond the classroom—and across borders.
